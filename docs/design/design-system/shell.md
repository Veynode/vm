# Workspace shell

Part of the [Veynode design system](../DESIGN.md). The columns and panes, every head and how they line up, the sidebar, compact and narrow layouts, and the desktop app's windows. Values come from the scales in [Foundations](foundations.md#6-spacing-sizes-radii-elevation-and-icons); the materials from [Visual direction](foundations.md#3-visual-direction).

## Workspace shell

Desktop structure is navigation → main conversation → optional details. The conversation and its composer come first; details can close. Settings and Space take the same columns (Settings is a page over the workspace, not a dialog: [Settings](settings.md)).

### Columns and panes

- **The window.** The canvas is the floor (`chat-app`: `h-dvh`, 8px gaps, 8px from the window's top, right and bottom edges). The sidebar stands on the floor flush with the window's left edge and has no fill of its own; its contents are inset 12px (`px-3`). The conversation and the details are panes (`chat-main`, `conversation-details`: `rounded-xl bg-surface`), separated by the 8px of floor between them, never by a border.
- **Widths.** The sidebar is 300px until the person drags it (220–420px, `ResizableSidebar`, saved per side); the Settings and Space lists keep the chats sidebar's width so switching never moves a column. The details pane opens at 300px (280–600px). The sidebar drags wherever there are two columns; the details pane only where it stands beside the conversation (compact and up). A drag never takes the conversation below what the window leaves it (`fitSidebarWidth`): with two columns, where the details float over it, the conversation keeps 300px.
- **The conversation column.** Messages, the header's capsules and the composer share one 800px column centred in the pane (the composer box is 832px less its 16px sides), so the header, the transcript and the composer read as one column ([Message layout contract](message-layout.md#current-presentation)).
- **The side pane.** The details pane and the editors it holds (a Bot's, a Group's, a Routine's) put what shows at rest on a 16px edge: text, fields, a control row's first label and an icon button's glyph; a ghost control's hover fill may reach past it. Its footer, where an editor has one, is 64px and transparent, and centres on the composer's line (below).
- **The bottom line.** A side editor's footer centres on the composer's line (a 48px composer 8px above the pane's foot), so the bottoms of the columns read as one line. The composer keeps its place; the footer's padding follows it.

### Heads

Every column starts with a head, and every head is a fixed height in every state: a notice, a search or a longer name never makes it taller or adds a line under it. **Their centres share one line**, 36px from the window's top (the 8px floor plus half a 56px row), so the titles across the columns stand on one line and, in the Mac app, the traffic lights stand on it too.

Rules every head keeps:

- **Fixed height, one line.** A head never wraps, never grows and never takes a second row. A long title is cut with an ellipsis (the whole name is available where it is edited).
- **Title at the start, actions at the end.** The title is the head's first text, after a Back where there is one; actions sit at the end in one run of equal-height controls. A head carries no ⓘ and no description.
- **Notices go into the head.** A condition about the area a head covers (the conversation's computer is busy, the workspace's primary is away, this node took over) is a badge or a capsule in that head, never a card or a line under it. The take-over and primary-away notices (`WorkspaceNotices`, `NoticeCapsule`) are capsules in the conversation header's band: an icon, its words cut to one line and one action, so the transcript is never covered and the head keeps its height; what the line cuts is in a card behind it. The primary-away capsule (`HomeOfflineCapsule`) is one line of fixed height (40px, 44px on a phone), tinted `state-waiting` with a pulsing dot (still under reduced motion), and has one button, Nodes; below a 470px pane it shortens to Offline with its words behind the ⓘ and drops the saved time; it fades in and out in 160ms, at once under reduced motion.
- **Nothing at rest.** A head draws no fill and no separator of its own while nothing is under it. Where content scrolls under it (the conversation, a Settings page), what stays readable floats as floating capsules ([Materials](foundations.md#3-visual-direction)), never a bar across the column.
- **Type by role.** A column's head (sidebar, conversation, side pane) is `title-3`; a page's or a dialog's title is `title-2`.

**One head rule.** The sidebar head's rule (title at the start, never centred) holds for every head: side editors put their title at the start after Back, as the Settings band does; the computer window's head is the same 56px row as a dialog's, with its title in `title-3`, so the Mac lights centre on it as they do in the main window.

### Sidebar

- **One frame.** The chats, Settings and Space columns are the same frame: no padding of their own, then `SidebarHead`, a list, the places control (`SidebarTabs`: one element for the whole page, `TabsHost`, which moves into the slot each place leaves, over the column's foot with the list running on under it (no edge cuts the list above it), and, on a phone, floats at the screen's foot over a list that scrolls to the edge; changing place moves only its disc, and no place has an entrance animation), and, only in a window with its own close, `SidebarFoot` (`packages/ui/src/settings/sidebar-head.tsx`; the list is `SidebarList` for pages and places, the chats' own virtual scroller for conversations), each on the same 12px inset. A side pane's 56px row is `PaneHead`, which `SidebarHead` is built on.
- **Rows.** Conversations are 56px rows (`conversation`: a 38px avatar, the name in `text-body-semibold`, the time in `caption-1`, a one-line preview in `body-2`, `rounded-md`); Settings pages and Space's places are 36px rows (`SettingsNavRow`: a bare 18px icon 12px before the name in `text-body-regular` `ink-2`, the shown one `text-body-medium` `ink`; 44px on a phone, the icon 20px centred in a 40px slot and every name `text-body-medium` `ink`, as a page's own rows). The current row takes the surface's fill and hover the quiet fill, in both lists and on glass alike ([Materials](foundations.md#3-visual-direction)); a conversation's weight never changes with the state. A row's state is the avatar's face or a dot ([Motion and feedback](motion-copy-accessibility.md#10-motion-and-feedback)); a preview never prefixes a state the avatar shows ([Conversation and composer](interactions.md#conversation-and-composer)).
- **Search.** An icon button in the chats' head opens the conversation finder (⌘K does the same); the list itself has no filter field and no search row.
- **Places.** Chats, Settings and Space are three equal icon segments in a capsule (`surface-2`, 40px tall, 32px segments) under every sidebar's head on the column edge. The place shown is a `surface` disc with its solid icon; the others are quiet with the hover fill. A place that needs the person carries the ink dot, described to assistive technology. On a phone the control is a centred floating capsule ([Materials](foundations.md#3-visual-direction)) over over the list's foot: three 44px discs in a 56px capsule 8px above the bottom edge, the shown one on `surface-2`; the list scrolls under it and ends 80px clear, so only the capsule takes pointer input. It hides while the keyboard is up.

### Compact and narrow

- **Compact** (below 950px): the details pane floats over the conversation from the right, 8px in, 280px wide, solid (`surface`) with `shadow-float`, opened from the header's toggle and closed from its own head.
- **Narrow** (below 600px): one column at a time, edge to edge with no corners: the list, then the conversation (Back in the header's lead), then the details as a page of their own. Settings' list and page take turns the same way. Each page deeper pushes a history entry (`narrowStep` in `conversation-address.ts`), so the system Back and the page's own Back go back over it; wider layouts replace the entry, so switching chats adds none. Every column is on `surface`, the lists as much as the pages, and so is the page's edge (one colour for the bars iOS Safari tints once at load). A page one deeper (a conversation, a Settings page or one under it, a Space place) comes in from the right over the one it leaves, which drifts a quarter left and darkens; Back is the same reversed (`pageTransition`, `packages/console/src/lib/page-transition.ts`: 350ms on the standard curve, the screen as two View Transition pictures, but the chats' list and conversation as the live panes with the swipe back's marks, because a picture ends at the page's foot and the list runs on under the browser's bottom bar; changing place, a correction of the address and reduced motion just change). A touch from the left 30px carries a page back to the list by hand (`useSwipeBack`, `packages/ui/src/boardui/overlays/swipe-back.tsx`): the list stands behind it from a quarter left under a veil, and a release past half way or a flick right goes back, otherwise the page springs back; on iOS, in a browser tab or a home-screen app, the system's own edge swipe goes back instead (both at once showed the list twice). A page under another page (a node's page, a file opened in Space) has only its Back for now. Editors open as a `Modal`. Heads keep 56px; touch targets in them grow to 44px.
- Never squeeze both full sidebars into a narrow window; keep every back and close path and the drafts.

### Phones and tablets in the browser and installed

The page draws edge to edge (`viewport-fit=cover`) and never scrolls, so the browser's toolbars change only when the person changes them. Source: `packages/console/src/styles/pwa.css`, `packages/console/src/styles/chrome-glass.css` (the floor), `packages/console/src/components/ui/ViewportFit.tsx`; the check is `tests/browser/ios-viewport-browser.mjs`.

- **Height.** One column's pane (`.chat-app`) is `100dvh` plus `--under-bar`, drawn through the bars: it starts at the screen's top (a negative top margin takes the status bar's inset back) and runs past the page's foot, where a browser's bottom bar floats over it and a home-screen app's viewport can stop short of the screen (`--under-bar` is 8rem, 0 under the soft keyboard). Measured in an installed app in the iOS 26 simulator (iPhone 18 Pro): the page is laid out in the screen less the status bar (`100%` is 812pt of 874pt) but drawn from the screen's top, and while nothing runs past that box the viewport shrinks to it, leaving a 62pt strip at the foot that nothing paints. An open conversation's body (`100%`, its own scroller) did exactly that, so the composer stood about 100pt above the foot. There the root and the body are `100lvh`, also while a field is in use, and the pane keeps that height under the keyboard (`dvh` shrinks): changing them at focus dropped the page about 40pt as the keyboard began to rise. Negative margins give the extra back and the root clips, so the page is no taller than the screen and never scrolls; the messages show through the bars to the screen's edges. The head (56px plus the top inset) and the composer (its offset is the bar, the home inset and its own gap) stand clear of them, and the list's padding follows: VirtualMessages takes its end inset from the composer's top to the list's foot. It follows a toolbar once and keeps the composer above it in either state; `svh` would leave a gap when the bar goes away, `lvh` hides the composer behind it while it shows. Nothing listens for `resize`. Measured in iOS 26 Safari: a composited layer and a fixed one stop at the page's foot, and an `overflow: auto` list paints past it only when nothing masks it. The top strip never shows page content, and bars fold away only when the document itself scrolls, which the shell avoids; the page scrolling for it was tried and given up.
- **Ground.** The strips the insets leave (status bar, the notch's sides, the home indicator, what a toolbar uncovers) carry the colour of what is beside them: one column spreads the pane's own fill outside it (a shadow, so the pane stays the size the content needs); a wider layout stands on the floor, which `html` carries too. A browser page has no wallpaper, frosted glass or window mode: the panes are plain surfaces on the floor and the app always fills the window (the Mac app's vibrancy is its own, below). `theme-color` follows the page's edge (`--theme-edge`) through `ViewportFit`; in one column an open conversation's edge is the surface itself (its pane is the surface, and iOS Safari tints its bars from the page's background), the chats list's the floor. In a home-screen app the status bar lies over the pane, so in an open conversation the top inset is the system's plus half a rem (the clock has air above the head) and a band of the surface's colour (`.chat-app.has-conversation::after`, the inset plus 3rem high, the colour solid over its first third and then one eased fade to nothing, no pointer events) keeps the messages scrolled under it from running into the clock and gives the system's blur a flat colour to sit on; the chats list and Settings have none.
- **Keyboard.** Nothing is resized for the soft keyboard, as in Framework7's Messages: iOS scrolls the page up with the keyboard until the page's end shows, so the composer and the rows above it rise with it and the head scrolls off. While a field is in use (`html[data-editing]`, set by `ViewportFit` on focus, before iOS decides how far to scroll) the pane no longer runs past the page's foot (`--under-bar` is 0), so the page ends at the composer's foot. On a phone the root and the body are the screen's height and the body is its own scroller, which no drag moves (`overflow: hidden`; at rest the pane past the page's foot let a drag that no list takes, one starting on the composer, carry the whole page up by it) (`pwa.css`): with the page's overflow on the root, iOS 26 Safari scrolled the composer about 100pt short of the keyboard. Only on an iPhone, while the keyboard covers more than 24px, `ViewportFit` also sets `data-keyboard` and `--keyboard-cover` (the part of the layout's foot the keyboard covers, read again at 80, 200, 400 and 800 ms) on `html`: a sheet stands above it, the places hide, and the command line's window pads its foot by it. While the keyboard is up a drag that no scrollable list takes is cancelled, so the page never scrolls under the list. When the pane changes height under it (the keyboard coming or going), the transcript keeps the line at the composer's top: a reader at the end stays at the end, and a reader in the history sees the same text above the composer with the keyboard open as before and after. The page's end then still lies the home inset below the composer, so the composer's fade turns `surface-2` and runs across the screen: a bar the composer stands in and the keyboard meets, as Framework7's message bar does (its bar keeps the same inset, about 50pt in the installed app). Measured in the iOS 26 simulator (Safari tab, beside the Framework7 kitchen sink): the composer stops right above the address bar and returns to its rest place when the keyboard goes. Measured in the iOS 27 simulator (installed app): closing pans the page with the keyboard, but opening does not; iOS starts its pan only once it reports the keyboard, 15 to 100 ms into the rise, and pans on a slower curve, so the composer is behind the keyboard for about 100 ms of the rise (Framework7's message bar for about 70 ms). The page draws no frame between the focus and that report, and the pan's progress never reaches the page, so the page cannot lead it. Android and a device are not measured.

### Desktop app

The desktop app shows the same console in its windows; the page knows where it runs from `data-runtime="electron"` and `data-platform` on `html`, and `data-fullscreen="true"` in full screen.

- **Glass (Mac).** The main window, Settings in it, the machine window and the setup window are frosted glass (`vibrancy: "sidebar"`, light or dark following the app's theme): the floor and the sidebar are left clear, the panes are 80% surface (the details 92%, solid again while they float at compact), and every fill on glass becomes a tint of the text colour, because an opaque grey on glass disappears ([Materials](foundations.md#3-visual-direction); the values are in `packages/console/src/styles/chrome-glass-desktop.css`). Dialogs, menus and tooltips stay solid. In the Mac window the panes' corners are `lg` (18px), concentric with the window's own; everywhere else they stay `xl`.
- **Title bar (Mac).** There is no title bar: the window's heads are its title bar. The traffic lights stand at the start of the sidebar head, centred on the line every head shares (`trafficLightPosition` in `apps/controller-desktop/src/windows.ts`), and the sidebar's title starts 84px in, after them. In full screen there are no lights and the title is back at 20px. Dialogs and sheets stand `--window-controls-inset` (36px) lower so the lights stay clear (none in full screen).
- **Dragging.** The sidebar heads, the conversation header and the exchange view's head drag the window; every button, link, field and menu inside them does not. A 10px strip along the window's top (`DesktopChrome`) drags where no head is.
- **The computer's window.** A computer opens in a window of its own (`/screen/<computer id>`): the dialog fills it and its header is the title bar, with the lights at its start (its title starts 84px in) and the window's own controls in place of fullscreen and close. Its lights are centred on the 56px head, as the main window's are, so the two windows put them at the same height.
- **The machine window** (the node's own page in the desktop app) is glass like Settings, with its own lights position for its own head.
- **Windows.** A 48px title bar the page draws (`.desktop-window-header`) carries the window's title and drag area beside the system's controls; the page stands under it (`--desktop-header-height`) and dialogs stand that much lower.
- Settings in the desktop app is the same page over the main window as on the web (⌘, and the tray open `/settings/<page>` there); there is no Settings window of its own.
