# Settings

Part of the [Veynode design system](../DESIGN.md). Settings pages, nodes, roles, plugins, channels, catalogs and empty states (section 9, second half).

This is the authoritative Settings spec. Settings is a sidebar of pages and one pane per page made of grouped rows, in the materials the chats stand in (Materials in [Visual direction](foundations.md#3-visual-direction)): the list on the floor, the page a pane, its groups the veil, and what floats over a scrolling page a floating capsule. The shell is `WorkspaceSettings` (`packages/console/src/components/settings/`) drawn with `SettingsPage` (`packages/ui/src/settings/settings-page.tsx`); the pages are the registry in `settings-pages.tsx`; the addresses are `packages/console/src/lib/console-routes.ts`. `pnpm check:design` enforces the parts marked _(checked)_.

### Shell

- **Sidebar.** The list of pages in the chats sidebar's own column (same width, resized the same way): `Settings` as its title (a `SidebarHead`, as every sidebar's: [Heads](shell.md#heads)), then the pages as one run of rows, Account first (a row like the others: no picture, name or email in the list): nothing sets one group of pages apart from the next unless it carries a small grey label. The rows stand on the floor like the chats, with no box round them: a row is the page's icon, 18px, bare and in the name's colour, 12px before its name, 36px tall with 10px each side, `sm` radius (on a phone 44px, `md` radius, the icon 20px centred in a 40px slot, and a chevron says it opens a page) (`SettingsNavRow`). Space's places are the same rows. A name is regular in `ink-2`; hover brings it and its icon to `ink`, and the shown page's name is medium in `ink`: unlike the chats, whose names keep one weight, the shown page stands out by weight and light as well as by its fill (on a phone, where nothing is marked, every name is medium in `ink`, as a page's own rows are). Hover is the quiet fill and the shown page's row takes the surface's fill, as the current chat does, on the Mac's glass too (on dark glass both take a lighter veil, the page's fainter than the chat's). The hover fill eases (100ms); the shown page's fill changes at once, with the page, never blending from the hover fill (on light glass the two lie on opposite sides of the floor, and a blend passes through no fill). A page with something waiting marks its row with a count chip or a dot whose words describe the row (`aria-describedby`) rather than rename it, so voice control still finds the page and colour is never the only sign. Under the list, the places as icons ([the sidebar's foot](shell.md#sidebar)).
- **Pane.** One page at a time, on the surface. A 56px title band over the page: Back when the page is nested (a round button whose arrow stands on the column's edge, a capsule of its own as the conversation's Back is), the page's title in `title-2` with no icon (a node's or a virtual machine's page puts the machine's icon before its name), and the page's actions at its end (`PanelHeader` portals them through `SettingsActionsSlot`; a page never draws its own header, title or back arrow). At rest the band draws nothing of its own and the title starts where the content starts; once the page has scrolled under it, Back, the title and the actions are each a floating capsule (the title moves to take its capsule's padding) (solid `surface`, `shadow-float`: the conversation header's), 40px tall (44px on a phone); words that lead the actions are inset 10px from its round end, and an end holding one icon alone is round, as wide as it is tall. The page's content is as wide as the band, at most 680px, and starts under it. Pages change at once, with no motion between them.
- **Content is fields, then grouped rows.** A page's root is the `settings-page-body` recipe _(checked)_: its parts 32px apart in body text. What the person fills in or picks from a list is a `Field`, written as in the Bot and routine editors (see Rows and controls); everything else is grouped rows. A group is one `SettingsGroup` box (the veil: `surface-2`, no edge, `lg` radius, rows padded 16px at the sides, dividers between rows that stop 16px short of each side), its rows 48px tall (`min-h-12`) and one line each, with an optional label above it (`SettingsSection` title) and an optional footnote 8px under it (`SettingsGroup footnote` or `SettingsSection description`, 13px, ink-2). **What is used comes first**: every group is drawn one way; a group that is set once and left alone, or only looked at, comes after the groups that are used; nothing is folded away. **A block has one label, written one way** (`BLOCK_LABEL`): the label over a field's control and the label over a group are the same 13px semibold in ink, 6px above the block; the label, the block and its footnote share one left edge, none of them inset. A label is never stacked on another: where the fields under a title carry labels of their own, the title is left out (General has none). A label's row is as tall as the buttons beside it (28px) and as tall as its words without any. Menu group names, table column heads and the sidebar's group names are not block labels and stay small and grey. **Every row leads with its icon, never on a plate.** A setting, an action or a way into a sub-page leads with a bare 20px line icon in `ink-2` (`SettingsRow icon`, or a `SettingsLinkRow` given one), the icon the sidebar gives a page where the row names the same thing (Models `Cpu`, Memory `Brain`, Skills & MCP `WandSparkles`, Permissions `ShieldCheck`). A thing in a list leads with its own mark at 32px: a computer's own icon, a third party's or a plugin's logo, a person's or a Bot's avatar; a thing without one has its line icon centred in that 32px slot (`ListRowIcon`), so the names of one list start on one edge. A group of things with their own marks takes no other icon; a row whose name is the words it keeps (a memory) takes none, and neither does a red action row (`SettingsActionRow`): its red word is the row. A list of things is a group of `ListRow`s or `SettingsLinkRow`s. There are no free-standing cards, tiles or panels: a box that is not a group is a group.
- **No introduction.** A page opens on its title, its actions and its first group. What a page or a group is for is said in a group's footnote, or behind an `InfoTip` beside the label it explains; never a paragraph at the top of a page.
- **Phone.** Below `narrow` the list and the page take turns: the page's Back returns to the list, and the places control to the chats. Rows keep their order; a row stays one line, its label wrapping and a long value cut; only a text box or a select moves under its label.
- **Window.** Settings covers the workspace (`settings-overlay`) and keeps Tab inside itself (`containFocus`) unless a dialog, menu or list opened over it owns the keyboard. Escape goes one level up, then from a page to the list (phone), then leaves Settings; a dialog left open underneath (a Bot editor Settings was opened from) never takes Escape or Tab from it. Closing returns focus to what opened Settings, or to the Settings button beside the person's name.

  An unknown address goes to the page it is under (Nodes for an unknown page). The retired addresses of the virtual machine pages lead to where their content went: `/settings/vms` and `/settings/nodes/<id>/machines` to Nodes or the node's page, with `vm=` to that virtual machine's page and `create=` to New virtual machine on Nodes; `/settings/nodes?node=&vm=` likewise.

- **At most two levels under a page**: page → item → the item's sub-page. Anything deeper is a sheet.
- **Back goes one level up** (`settingsParent`): a page under a node to the node, a node or a connection to its page, a step of adding a connection to the choice of service, a sign-in to its connection. A page whose Back must do more (drop a form's draft, return to the page that sent the person) puts its own `back` in its heading (`setHeading`); `backDisabled` holds Back and Escape while something is saved. The browser's Back walks the history.
- **One row opens a sub-page**: `SettingsLinkRow` (an optional icon, the label, a state, then at the row's end what it is (`meta`), an optional value and a chevron, all on one line; a row that ends in a ··· menu draws no chevron beside it; a press anywhere on it opens it, while its status and its own buttons stay pressable). A row whose name opens a details dialog is the same row. Never an Open or Set up button in a row, never a row that is a raw button.
- Copy that sends the reader into Settings names the page as the sidebar does (Settings → Nodes).

### Rows and controls

- **Something to fill in is a `Field`**, the editors' own: a `Select`, a text or number box or a picker under its label (13px semibold, an ⓘ beside it for an explanation), at the default control size (36px), as wide as the page's column and straight on the pane, never inside a group. Fields stand 16px apart at the top of the page, before the groups; one a switch reveals follows that switch's group. A picture is the page's head, centred with its buttons under it, as a Bot's is in its editor.
- **A setting that is on or off, a value or an action is a `SettingsRow`**: its label on the left (an ⓘ for an explanation), and at the right end, centred on the row, a `Switch`, a small `Button`, a value, or an input (a `Select`, a text box or a number box, such as a port beside its 28px Save) that shrinks with the row. The row centres and keeps the size of what it holds; never re-centre it by hand _(checked)_.
- **Controls that share a line share a height.** A line of controls (the tabs, search box and filter over a list; the title band's actions; a row's own controls) is one size throughout: 36px (`control-md`) for a field, a filter line and the title band, 28px (`control-sm`) for a row's button, 44px on a phone. A tab strip, a search box, a `Select` and a `Button` side by side never differ in height, and they share one radius family (pills with pills). **Current:** a row's ··· (`RowMenu`) is 32px beside 28px buttons; a page's pencil (`EditButton`) is 36px in the title band; the runtime update rows use `medium` buttons (`RuntimeUpdateSettings.tsx`). These follow the [size scale](foundations.md#sizes) once its proposal is decided.
- **A row is one line**: its name, one state (`StateTag`: a badge, its word in a 20px pill whose colour says the state: green for fine, orange for waiting on the person, red for failed, grey for a fact such as a role and for work under way; a ring while busy), what it is or is set to at its end (`meta`, `value`), with at most one direct control (a switch or the main action); what is done now and then goes in the row's ··· `RowMenu`, which hands focus back to ··· when a dialog it opened closes; a right click on the row opens the same menu. A row keeps its 48px at every width: short of room its meta goes first, then its name is cut, and its state stays (a dot only where even the name at 48px does not fit beside it); nothing moves under it. A line that says nothing is happening (no Bot uses it) is left out. A list offers a search box only from six items; Skills & MCP shows its search box always.
- **More is shown on request** through a `SettingsSection` whose heading says what it is behind an ⓘ and whose button opens the rest, or through a sheet; never a raw `<details>` _(checked)_.
- **A list the person builds in a form** (port forwards, DNS servers when each is its own entry) is a `SettingsSection` named for it, an ⓘ beside the name for the format, **Add** as a small secondary button with its `+` in the heading, and one group under it: each entry a 48px row holding its value typed in place (no box of its own; mono for addresses and ports) and its remove ×, with `None` in `ink-2` while there are none. Add puts a new empty row at the end and focuses it. Never a ghost `+ Add …` button under the inputs, and never a box per entry.
- **A dialog says a consequence once, in its verb.** Where saving also restarts, sends or replaces something, the footer's button says so (Save and restart) and nothing above the fields repeats it. A `Notice` in a dialog is for a condition that stops the form (a running system to shut down first, with its action), never for the effect of submitting.
- **Creating opens a sheet**: a `Modal` with its fields in `dialog-body` and Cancel and the verb in `DialogFooter` (a memory, a virtual machine, a password). A flow of several steps that is a place of its own (signing in a ChatGPT account, adding a model service) is a sub-page whose actions stay pinned at the pane's foot with no fill and no capsule of their own, the fields scrolling above them (`SettingsFormPage`). An item's own page may hold its fields as a draft with Save pinned at the foot (a node's settings).
- **Destructive actions are the last group** of the page: a red text row (`SettingsActionRow`) that asks first through a `ConfirmDialog` naming what goes, with what it does said in the group's footnote (Delete this account, Remove from account, Delete). A thing's page in Skills & MCP and an adapter plugin's row put Uninstall in their ··· menu instead.
- **Drafts.** These half-written forms survive switching pages and closing Settings, which unmount them, because their drafts live in a memory-only workspace atom (`useSettingsDraft`, `packages/console/src/state/workspace.tsx`): the name on Account, a memory being written, edited or merged, and the form for another model service. Where Settings is lives in the address, not in a draft. The New computer form keeps its fields in the sheet and starts over. A draft is never written to browser storage and never holds a credential or key; a successful save, an explicit Cancel or a step back to the list clears it. Closing asks nothing.

### Loading, empty and failed

The states are [Components](components.md#loading-empty-and-failed): while a page loads it is the page itself with a bar for each value not read yet; `EmptyState` when there is nothing; `PanelLoadError` when its data did not load; a refresh that fails keeps the content and says so once.

### Pages

- **Entry.** Settings opens from its segment in the places control under the sidebar's head (`data-settings-trigger`); on a phone it is its own page, so Back closes it. Every page before the workspace uses one shell, `AuthPage` (`packages/console/src/components/account/AuthPage.tsx`). On a node's own page a person at the machine itself is the owner and sees no sign-in; a visitor from another device signs in with the email and password of the account the node belongs to; a node in no account answers nobody beyond its own machine. There is no access-token screen. The account's web app signs in to the account, opens the account's key with the password and works on the account's primary node; with no node yet it shows Add node and waits. A reset password leaves a key nothing opens: the page says so, offers Start the key over behind a confirmation, then one login line per node. First-run setup is one page, `/setup` (see [Pattern catalog](components.md#pattern-catalog)).
- **Notifications** (on General): until the browser has been asked, a secondary Turn on notifications asks (a browser asks only from a click). Once allowed, a Select chooses per device (`notifyAtom`): When a Bot replies or needs you, Only when a Bot needs you, or Off. Off sends no system notification, and the tab title and conversation list still show what waits. A reply that was not announced still counts as seen. It is one row, first in the group Sounds is in: the Select, the button or the state sits at its end. A browser that blocked them says so in an orange tag (the person must act) with where to allow them under the group; an unsupported browser the same in grey; the macOS app says where its own setting lives behind an ⓘ.
- **Models** (`SettingsView`) lists the connections by where they run (services reached from here, then each machine's), each a `SettingsLinkRow` that opens the connection's page, with Add in the title band. A row's status shows only when it asks something of the person or a check is under way (`statusShown`): a connection that works says nothing, and Default is its only tag. Add opens the choice of connection, one group of two rows (ChatGPT first where the host signs one in, then API), each opening its form as a sub-page. The API form asks, in order, the API format (a pill segmented control: OpenAI, the default, Claude, Gemini), the address (a select of that format's services, each with its address under its name, and last Custom address, which shows a URL box under it; another format goes back to its first address), the API key, the model and an optional name. A service in the list is reached through Pi's own support for it; a custom address in the chosen format. A model service's connection is a service and its key; the model it keeps is only checked with, listed by the service once the key is in (before that the Model field says so, disabled), and its row and page name the address, never a model, because each Bot picks its own in its form (Connection, then Model: the models the service lists, searched from seven, each with the efforts it thinks at, or one typed by hand; a Bot is not saved on a model service without one). On a connection's page the head names the service, and the form keeps the key, a custom address and the model; the format and the service are not changed there. The form has no Advanced: Pi knows a listed service's windows and image support, and a check of a custom address finds whether its model reads images. A connection's page is built as a node's: the title band says Models (where Back leads), and the head (`ConnectionPageHead` on `PageSummary`) is the service's logo, the connection's name (said there and nowhere else), Default and a status only when it asks something, and the address, or for a ChatGPT account its email and plan, with a ··· menu at its right end (Check connection, Set as default, Sign out for an account, Delete apart) and the pencil (its name, in a sheet) in the title band; Default never pushes the address under it, which is cut instead. Under it come what fixes it when something is wrong (signing in again, allowing the computers that wait), a ChatGPT account's usage, its settings, whose Check connection and Save show only while something differs from what is saved, and the Bots that use it, one line each with the model it runs (a ChatGPT account's default by name for a Bot that picked none), a press opening that Bot's editor over Settings; past 40 they scroll in a box of their own and only the rows in view are drawn (`VirtualRows`). A connection's status is Connected or Connection error from its latest check; a check of an edited draft leaves the saved status alone. Detect models fills the Model field in place, which becomes the list. A Run that fails for a connection's fault (a rejected key, a service that did not answer) also sets that connection to Connection error with the reason, until a later check or Run goes through; refusals, limits and content errors say nothing of the connection. The failure's recovery actions lead to it with Open Models.
- **Account** (`AccountPage`): its head is the person's initial and name, with a pencil in the title band that renames in a sheet; then how they sign in, the browsers signed in (`SignedInBrowsers`, where the host lists them: this one tagged, Sign out on the others), and leaving or deleting last.
- **About**: versions (Veynode is the product version a release bumps, `VITE_VEYNODE_VERSION`), diagnostics to copy, and one Shortcuts row that opens the list (`ShortcutsRow`); the shortcuts are not a page. Where the console is the node's own, its update section follows and the first group has no Updates row. The update section (`RuntimeUpdateSettings`, also on a node's page) is three one-line rows; a copy that does not update itself is one Version row tagged Manual updates. Version says an installed update once: a waiting Restart pending tag (its `DetailTip` says when it restarts, or the `veynode restart` command to copy where Veynode cannot restart itself), the running and installed versions, and Restart now in Check for updates' place, which asks first while tasks run; Restarting… is a working tag. Last check is its time, tagged Up to date or Failed (the reason in the tag's `DetailTip`). Then Update automatically.
- **Memory** (`AgentMemory`) opens on the memory keeper's row (its model and first trigger on a line under its name, the one Settings row with a second line; when it last tidied, 整理中 as a badge while it does), then a search box from six memories (16px from its neighbours), then Waiting for your review, then Remembered. A memory a Bot notes is in use at once, and the keeper's tidy applies what it finds; only what a channel's chat brings waits. Waiting holds what Bots want to remember from one (Remember, Don't remember; Replaces one when it would replace a memory), memories somebody there asked a Bot to forget (Delete, Keep; {name} asked to forget) and what an older node's keeper proposes (Merge, Don't merge with Merges N; Delete, Keep with Expired): each row shows its words whole up to three lines with its two answers at the first line (✕ and ✓ in a row under 440px: a phone, or details beside a wide list), its badges under them; more than four show three and an N more row, with Remember none and Remember all beside the heading. A remembered memory is one fixed-height line: its words cut, where it is used (a group's name or Task, grey with its icon) and when, Expired as a badge, and a ··· menu (Edit, Delete) a right click opens too. A press opens a row whole as a sheet: its words, scope, source conversation and time, who said it when not the person, the source's words and earlier versions, a pending item's answers, a merge's memories. The keeper's sheet chooses its model as a Bot's form does and its triggers as a Routine's (fixed time and fixed interval only; none, only when asked), lists its recent tidies, and has Save and Tidy now. Add is in the title band, with a ··· menu: Tidy now, Merge by hand (the band then says how many are ticked, with Cancel and Merge, which opens the merge sheet) and Export all (one Markdown file). The keeper is not a Bot and is listed nowhere else.
- **Nodes** (`NodesSettings`) is the one page for nodes and virtual machines, in two flat parts: the nodes (`nodeSections`: this node first, then the account's other nodes, the ones with no record yet, and what is left of nodes that are gone), then, under the heading Virtual machines, every virtual machine. There is no separate page of virtual machines, no expandable row, no row set in under another and no management dialog. **Every row is one line**: an icon, the name, at most two tags and its ··· menu, with no chevron beside the menu (a row with nothing to do, this node's while no record stands for it, ends empty); no second line, no line under the row. The menu (`nodeRowMenu`, `vmRowMenu` in `packages/console/src/components/settings/nodes/row-menu.ts`), the same on a right click, holds Open desktop, Edit, the role changes, a virtual machine's Restart, and last what removes it. **A node's row** shows its state with no word where its icon can: Online is a green dot at the lower right of its icon, and Offline (this node cut off from the account too) is its icon in grey; only Paused, Needs setup and Removed are tags (`nodeStatus`). Beside them is a role tag (Primary, or Standby with the copy's state; none for an ordinary node); the node this page is on says This machine at its end on a page the machine serves, and nothing through the relay. A node that is online and needs something of its person says Needs attention in place of Online; what it needs is on its page. It has no button. On the node's page each reason is a notice with the action that fixes it (Finish setup, Open permissions). **A virtual machine's row** shows what is under way or wrong and a role tag once it has a role; on, stopped or offline is its icon, in colour or grey, with no word for it (`ComputerStatus quiet`). Where the account has more than one node its node is a mark on its icon, never a name at its end. Why it is off is on its page. A virtual machine given a role says that role from the moment it is given. The ones being made (with the time left and Cancel), the ones a host reports without a record, and the ones this page only reads are rows of the same part. The search box appears only from six rows. A node in no account says so with Sign in or create an account; one put out of its account says why with Sign in again. **Add a node** is the title band's last button, after Check and update all (a refresh icon that spins while every online node checks for a newer Veynode and each that installed one and runs no task restarts into it, the primary last; a toast says only a failure or that all are up to date); a virtual machine is made from its node's page or from New VM in the Nodes list's section head, which opens the form at once on the first node that can take one, its Node field choosing another (`NodeList.tsx`; the `create=` address opens it there). **Every action has one home.** A press on a row opens its page (`/settings/nodes/<id>`); its ··· menu is a shortcut to what that page does. **Both pages are built alike**, with no head of their own: the title band names the machine, its icon (32px; the green dot of another node that is online) before its name, and its last control is Edit as a pencil on a node's page and the ··· menu its row has on a virtual machine's (Edit inside it). The name is said there and nowhere else; no tag says what system it runs, and Online is the dot. Under the band, the things to press are up to three alike tiles (`PageActions`: an icon over its word, each a third of the line), then what the machine needs from its person as notices with the action that fixes it (`NodeAlertLines`), then groups of rows. A part of the page that did not load is one row, Didn't load with its ⓘ and Retry (`PanelLoadError row`); only a page that has nothing to show keeps the large one. The name is changed, with the icon, from Edit in a sheet; Save closes it and the new name and icon show everywhere at once, the old ones coming back with a toast if the save fails (`saveComputerLook`). **A node's page** (`NodePage`) has the tiles Desktop and Terminal (no Desktop on the node this page is served by), then its Role row on every node of the account: the role word as a picker (`RolePicker`: Primary, Standby, Node for an ordinary one) whose menu holds the role changes, a short sync tag beside it only while a standby's copy is not up to date, and, where nothing can be changed from this page, the word in grey with why behind an ⓘ. Then Virtual machines, one group: its virtual machines as the Nodes list's rows (with New virtual machine as a plus in the section's title, off while the node cannot take one, with why behind an ⓘ), or while it has none New virtual machine as a row of its own (a dashed plus), and last a Settings row whose line sums up what is set (the plugins installed, the limits) and opens the page of their settings (`/settings/nodes/<id>/vm-settings`, titled Virtual machines). That page holds, on another node, Allow virtual machines (a switch) and while on its limits, each a row with − and + that saves on its own; the node's virtual machine plugins (installed, installing with Cancel, Install, Needs setup); and on the machine the Controller runs on Downloaded system files (a row that opens the list sheet) and the files waiting for cleanup. A node that runs none yet lists, in the section, the plugins that would let it, each installed right there, over New virtual machine greyed; a node that is itself a virtual machine says it makes none. The private network is, on this node, the switch that joins it; another node's says no state, only Install while it needs a command run to join; its domain and address follow only while it is on; another node with nothing to say about it is one row, Private network · Off. Then Models and the Veynode row (the program's update, behind an ⓘ, with Restart to update when one is waiting), the Updates group, this node's own pages (storage, permissions, the desktop window), and last one group: Allow control (the Controller's access to it, with what turning it off does behind its ⓘ) and its removal as a red row. While no record stands for this node's machine its page keeps what needs none. **A virtual machine's page** (`VmPage`) has its power as the first tile (Stop while it is on, Start while it is off), then Desktop and Terminal; why its power is off is a sentence under the tiles. Below, one group of facts: its Role row where it is a node of the account or can become one (the same picker as a node's page, whose menu holds the role changes; its ··· menu then holds none), its system, a Status row only where the state says more than the power does (starting and stopping are the power tile's spinner alone), its node only where the account has more than one, its connection only while that is changing or its access was taken away (not while it starts or stops), its image, when it stops while unused, and its Veynode program. Its processors and memory are under Size, Snapshots (how many are used beside the title; one group: each snapshot with Restore and its ··· holding Clone and Delete, then a last row with the name box and Create) and Backups where its engine does them (one another node runs shows them once that node has said what its engine does, and nothing is asked of an offline node). Processors and memory are changed on this page and nowhere else. A virtual machine is not a node of the account until it is given a role: nothing says so, and Set as standby (on the Controller that holds it, while it runs) joins it and names it a standby in one step, without a question.
- **Roles.** This node's role is one line on its page (primary: your Bots and conversations run from it; standby: keeps a copy of the primary and can take over; ordinary: Bots can use it and your data stays on the primary). An account with a primary and no standby says Bots stop if the primary goes offline. Role actions (`NodeRoleMenu`, rules in `nodeRoleActions`, `packages/console/src/data/node-roles.ts`): Set as standby, Remove standby, Take over earlier / later (ask nothing: they change only who takes over first) and Make primary. The words name the role without "node", since a virtual machine can hold one and is always called a virtual machine. They are offered in the row's ··· menu and in the Role picker on the page of the node or virtual machine. The others ask first and say what they do to that node: making a standby deletes its conversations and settings and puts a copy of the primary's in their place; removing a standby empties it; making a node primary moves where Bots and conversations run, makes the current primary a standby and does not copy its virtual machines. A refusal is said in the dialog. Making a node primary is not waited for in its dialog: once confirmed the dialog goes, that node's Role row says Switching (`RoleSwitchTag`; Copying, with Cancel, while a node that asked for itself makes its first copy), no role action is offered meanwhile, and a refusal is a toast. The primary's own page, and a Controller that is itself a standby, offer no role actions. A standby's page carries `StandbyNotice` (standby for {name}, when it last synced or that it is making the first full copy, the sync problem).
- **Adding a node** (`AddNode`) is one dialog wherever it starts, over the Nodes list in Settings, opened in place with no page motion (the address `/settings/nodes/add` opens it and gives way to `/settings/nodes`): a system switch (macOS · Linux, Windows), then a line saying where to paste with, at its end, Login code (copies the code alone, for a node whose own page asks for it) and the code's countdown; under it, in no group, the command on one line with the login code cut to its ends and Copy as an icon (`CommandLine`). There is no way to make a new code by hand: one that runs out is replaced by itself. The box keeps one height while a code is made (a skeleton) and when none could be (the reason and Retry). Windows (preview) gets two numbered lines, Install Veynode with Download and the code itself to paste. A development page adds the real installer's command under a short line of its own. A line works once and for 10 minutes; the dialog may be closed once the line is copied, and has no Cancel beside its own close. Nobody approves the node: the line carries the account's key sealed under a secret only the line holds. The node's future row waits under the command as a dashed placeholder; when a node of the person's signs in (new, or listed offline before and online now) the command gives way to the node's row saying Joined, with a switch that makes it a standby where that applies and the way to its models, and Done. A Bot is given a node on the Bot's own form, never here. A failure is said in words and the node stays added. There is no connection code to paste and no second path for a machine that is "only a computer".
- **This node, on This machine** (`ThisNodeAccess`): two blocks on This machine, each under one label, drawn only in a browser at the machine itself: not in the desktop app (its Desktop page holds the port and who can open it), not through the relay, and nothing at all, no notice, for a browser on another device. **Access** (`LanAccessSettings`) comes first under the head, in two groups. The first is what takes something away, with one line under the group that says what: Pause this machine (the machine program's own pause, `/api/node/paused`, only from the machine: no Bot uses it; the row stands only where a machine program answers) and Node only (on, the node's own page serves no chats and it is otherwise the same node: its page, on any address and from another device after sign-in, is Settings alone, listing what the machine offers (This machine, Virtual machines, Storage, the models it serves itself), Account, Appearance and About, with no chats-and-Settings foot under the list and no way out of it; permissions, the private network, updates and virtual machines work as on any node, and a primary that is a node only still serves the account's web app and its other nodes; turning it off brings the chats back). The second is who reaches the console and where it is served: Allow access from my network (visitors sign in to the account; over http a notice says the page is not encrypted and passkeys do not work), connect to my other nodes directly on this network, and Port (the `NumberField` stepper in the row, Save before it only while the value differs; off when nothing restarts Veynode). Changing network access restarts Veynode for a few seconds and the page reconnects by itself. Saving a port restarts it too: the page waits under the applying notice and goes on at the same page on the new port; a port outside 1024 to 65535, one in use, or one that never answered is said under the group. **Workspace** (`NodeWorkspace`), after the private network, is the folder Bots work in on this machine: a monospace text box as wide as the column with Save in it only while it differs; a Bot at work there or a folder the machine refuses is said under the box (the machine's own reason behind a detail mark). It is not drawn where no machine program answers. **Permissions** (`ThisNodePermissions`), before the workspace on a Mac whose program has permissions to give: Accessibility and Screen Recording, each a row with its state (green Allowed, orange Not allowed yet) and Allow only on one that is missing; after the workspace, one row restarts the desktop program (`ThisNodeRelaunch`), which a new Screen Recording grant needs, and a Bot at work there is said under it.
- **Takeover of the primary** is spoken by three surfaces, each from real state (`GET /api/nodes/roles`), never a guess. (1) In the account's web app, when the primary does not answer (`BrowserGate`): the workspace stays on screen and a one-line capsule (`HomeOfflineCapsule`) says it. With a ready standby online it counts down to the standby taking over and continues on the new primary by itself; with a standby that is not ready it says so, the reason behind its ⓘ; with no standby online it says the page continues when the primary is back. Its one button opens Settings → Nodes, where Make primary on an online standby (behind a confirmation saying what is lost) takes over now or anyway and Add a node still works. While the primary is away Settings keeps Account, Nodes and Preferences; the pages that ask the primary are greyed, with no reason written on each row (a screen reader still hears it). Other nodes going offline never raise the capsule. (2) On the new primary a notice at the top of the workspace until dismissed (`TakeoverNotice`): after an automatic takeover when and from whom, how far the copy had reached and that recent changes may be missing; after a planned switch that nothing was lost; how many Runs were interrupted; and, while the node has Bots, Move Bots to this node, with the result said in the notice. (3) A primary that stopped work because it lost the account service refuses changes until it is back; a virtual machine on it reads Its node is not taking changes right now. None of this is the person taking over a desktop from a Bot.
- **Engines, models, lifecycle on a node.** A virtual machine runs on an engine (Apple container, Tart, WSL, Docker) only through that engine's adapter plugin: while the plugin is not installed the computer's one status is Needs the {name} plugin (`needsPlugin`), its ⓘ says its files are kept, and the row's action is the plugin's own (Install, Update, progress) in place of Start, with no Stop. New virtual machine lists, in its Plugin field, only the plugins installed on the chosen node (its engines and the plugins of the systems it can make now), never a list of every plugin that exists, and offers only the systems of the one chosen. A node with none installed shows no field: it says so and lists the virtual machine plugins that node can run, each installed right there (the same rows as the node's page), and the form goes on once one is; when Veynode's plugin server is not answering it says that instead, with Retry. An engine whose plugin is installed but that does not come up (`container-required`) keeps one line with the engine's answer behind an ⓘ. A lifecycle action pends on its own row in its own words (Starting…, Stopping…) and says its failure there while other rows stay usable. Virtual machines offer Stop while running and Delete once stopped or after a failed start (the row says why). Deleting a computer says what goes with it, one catalog sentence per applicable consequence (the person's own Bots left with no computer, how many enabled Routines on it will be turned off: `deleteConsequences`); the dialog shows its own failure in its `error` slot once. Preserve deletion confirmation and the per-node access switch. A computer running an older Veynode Computer than expected says so in its editor and the computer window (`ComputerRuntime`); Restart and update asks first (`ConfirmDialog`), because a restart ends every program on it.
- **Virtual machines** are rows of Nodes, each with a page of its own (see Nodes); the Bot's Computer field lists the same nodes and virtual machines, grouped by node with each one's state. Creating is one form, a sheet over the page (`CreateVirtualMachine` in a `Modal`): Node (the node it was opened on, in a select where a node that cannot take one says why), System (and the engine where more than one can make it), Name, Size (Processors, Memory, and Disk where the system has one). The fields say what Create makes; no line repeats them. The same request works on every node: another node chooses its engine, installs its tool and prepares its image. There is no Add host or managed-host flow, and a virtual machine cannot create virtual machines (its node's page says so). A node without a virtualization tool says so with Install a virtual machine plugin. Whether a node takes virtual machines is its owner's: creating the first one on a never-allowed node turns it on; turned off on the node itself it is disabled and says to turn it on there. A virtual machine on another node is a reference: it stays where it was made and is never copied when the primary changes. When its node cannot be asked the row says why and what still works: offline (cannot be started, stopped or opened; deleting removes only the record), Checking with the node (Veynode asks again and never creates it twice), removed from the account (only the record is left), not taking changes right now, too old to be asked, or has not accepted this node as the primary yet. Its desktop connects the browser straight to the node and shows frames relayed through the primary when no direct path opens. Downloaded system files open as a list sheet from the page of a node's virtual machine settings, whose rows each have Delete… in their ··· menu (none on a file a VM uses) behind a `ConfirmDialog`. The Nodes pages own their sheets and destructive confirmations (`VmConsoleDialogs`) under the [mandatory dialog motion contract](boardui.md#mandatory-dialog-motion-contract). Nothing invents progress: a phase, percentage or remaining budget renders only from server fields. An action the server reports unavailable renders disabled with its reason as visible text, never a hover-only tooltip. Detached local computers with unfinished physical deletion appear under Pending file cleanup on the page of the virtual machine settings of the node that held them, with their stored name and original backend; Retry cleanup… asks before deleting remaining files and warns that running programs may stop; a failed retry keeps the entry; opening or refreshing the page never starts cleanup; binding failures direct the person to restore the original environment and check again, or detach the computer; the UI never chooses a replacement engine for an existing computer.
- **Skills & MCP** (`CapabilityHub`, the page id `capabilities`) is the one page for what a Bot can be given: skills and MCP, alone or in plugins; sources, packages, versions and transports stay under a row's Advanced. What runs on a machine (virtual machine engines, model providers, messaging channels) is installed on the page that uses it (a node's page, its Models, Channels), never here. Two pill tabs, Installed (shown first) and Discover, and no filter by kind. The title band holds Add alone (nothing while a detail is the page; a ghost Add from URL, where every other page's Add is the primary `SettingsAddButton`), and the page is never switched to a node, because skills and MCP belong to the account; one row under the tabs holds the tab's own search box (each tab keeps its own words). Skills built into Veynode are not listed here (a Bot's editor lists them). Installed and Discover are the same list: one group of one-line `SettingsLinkRow`s, each a mark, a name and one tag that says what it is (Skill, MCP, or what a plugin brings, such as 4 skills). An Installed row adds Update available with an Update button and ends in a ··· menu (Details, and apart, Uninstall plugin; the confirmation says what goes with it); a Discover row adds one line of what it is for, its meta (`DiscoverRow`): it gives way first, so on a phone it is a stub or nothing and the name and tag stay whole (its words are the title and the row opens what Install does); it ends in Install, or Installed. Nothing is switched on or off here: which Bots get a thing is chosen in each Bot's settings. A Discover row opens what Install does, the preview of what will be added, which is its details: there is no page for a thing not installed; one already installed opens its page on Installed. An Installed row opens the thing's own page (`CapabilityHead`): the name is the title band's alone; under it the mark, the tag and one sentence, with Update and a ··· menu (Uninstall plugin) at the row's end; then for an installed plugin the settings it asked for (`PluginSettings`: a secret one such as a token is typed again to replace it and never shown, any other is only read), the items it brought as rows (each says Skill or MCP and at most one status that asks something of the person, nothing when it works), each opening its editor, and last Version and Source; a search that finds a plugin through an item shows the plugin with those names on its line. A row's editor holds on or off, Give to all Bots by default, who uses it and Give to a Bot…, sign-in, the computer an MCP runs on, Add another account, and Advanced. Discover lists the plugin server's cards as one virtual list in the pane's scroll that brings the next page as its end nears (each row is 56px at every width and the list's estimate is 56px, so its height is rows times 56 and does not move as rows are drawn; the place the skeleton gives to the list never closes, and a search that is still paging through empty pages keeps its skeleton); an entry for reference only shows why with Install off; one Veynode cannot sign in to yet (`needsOfficialClient`) is not listed; a plugin server that did not answer is one quiet line; once every entry is in and no search is active, one quiet More plugins are coming soon line ends Discover. Install from a card opens straight on the plugin's own dialog, never the Add box. Connecting an account opens the service's sign-in page (the system browser on desktop) and, where Connect was, says it waits with the page to open again and Cancel, checks back when the window regains focus and gives up after ten minutes in words; a sign-in that cannot start says the service's reason there (`InlineError`). Connecting an account, updating and uninstalling each toast. Add (`AddAnything`) is one box for a link, an MCP address, a config snippet or a ZIP; the server reads it and the dialog asks only what is needed (which plugin of a marketplace, which skills of a repository, the computer a program runs on, secrets typed like passwords when sensitive), previews what will be added with what is left out in one grey line and a warning when it is not a reviewed entry, installs, and last asks which Bots get it (none keeps the defaults). An update a reviewed entry can apply on its own never shows; any other shows Update available and opens what changes. Older page ids (`skills`, `mcps`, `plugins`) open this page.
- **Adapter plugins** are listed where they are used, each list only while it has rows: a node's page lists the virtual machine plugins that node can run, installed or not (installing never makes a row leave the list it was pressed on), and New virtual machine the same rows while the chosen node has none; a place's Models page, while it has no model provider, the providers it can run; Channels the channels installed, then those installed nowhere yet (Available). Kinds come from `GET /adapter-plugins`. Each is one `AdapterPluginRow` (`@veynode/ui/settings/adapter-plugin`), the same row the machine's own page draws: the kind's icon, the name with an ⓘ, one meta line (what is going on, phase and bytes while installing or why it failed, and at rest nothing, what the plugin does being on its own page, or, while its program is missing and cannot be installed, the entry's steps for this system), at most one state tag (Failed, Needs setup, Offline, Update the computer; an update waiting has no tag), then actions in one order: Install (also for a program already on the machine), a disabled Installing/Updating button (where that state is said) with a progress ring and Cancel, Retry after a failure, Update (subtle) and the ··· menu holding Uninstall alone. **Installed is not usable**: a plugin that drives a program (Docker, WSL, Tart, Apple container, Ollama) is usable only when that program is found in a supported version; until then its row says Needs setup (reason in the tag's `DetailTip`) with one way forward (Install program where Veynode can install it, otherwise the entry's steps as plain text in the page's language), Learn more and Check again (also runs when the window returns to front). The same steps appear as This computer needs first in the install dialog and the plugin's details. A plugin that needs setup is neither the engine for new computers nor offered as one. There is no default engine and no row tag or button about it: the New computer form's engine field (only while more than one is usable) decides per computer, else the platform's own engine when usable, else the first usable. An engine's service has a switch only where Veynode installed the program and its adapter can start and stop it (Apple container, Ollama) on the Controller's own machine: on unless turned off, turning on starts it at once, turning off asks first (Turn off {name}?, naming and stopping the running computers made with it first, main action Stop and turn off) and keeps it off until turned on; a program the person installed, Docker, WSL and Tart show no switch. A computer whose start failed because its engine is off says so and offers Turn on in place of Start; Veynode never turns an engine back on by itself. A plugin server that did not answer is a `Notice` above the groups with Retry. With one node in the list a row is that node and names none; with several, every plugin is its own row, each node it is on is a row under it (named, with its state and its own actions) and Install is a menu of the nodes it can still go on. One machine is one place: the machine Veynode runs on is This Mac once, never again under its computer's name, so installing there goes through the Controller; that computer's machine page lists no plugins and says they are installed on its page in Settings → Nodes. A machine with no Controller beside it manages its own, and while its access is paused nothing on it can be installed or removed. A program installed some other way carries no tag, is used as it is and never uninstalled. A failure is the Failed tag with the reason on the row's line, the machine's sentence in the `DetailTip`, and Retry, except a computer that stopped answering mid-install, which offers Check again. A reviewed entry installs with no dialog; anything else (pasted into Add, a third-party update) first shows Install {name}?: Unverified publisher, where it goes, each file with size and SHA-256, the adapter command, how its program is installed and what runs after, then Install anyway. Uninstall asks and says one sentence per applicable consequence from the Controller's plan (`GET /adapter-plugins/:id/uninstall-plan`, so what is said is what is done): the plugin and its data are removed from there, plus the program copy Veynode installed in its own folder; for a channel its connections (number), tokens and chats are deleted; what stays (a program the person installed, a system installer's program and its service, and for a backend the computers made with it, which keep their files). Running computers made with it are named and stopped first (main action Stop and uninstall); one that cannot be stopped fails the whole uninstall with nothing deleted. A model provider whose program answers lists its models on that computer, none ticked, with Select all; only ticked ones are deleted; for a program the person installed one line warns the models may not have been downloaded by Veynode; unticked models stay and the dialog says how many; with none listed or unlistable the dialog has no model section. First run offers the recommended entries that work on the Controller's machine, each ticked and skippable, installing in the background.
- **Messaging channels** (the Channels page) are listed there once installed, each with Manage, and under them those not installed yet, each with Install; with none installed the page is that list alone. They go on the Controller's machine only and what they may do is agreed first: Install always opens Install {name}? with What it asks to be allowed (receive messages, which can start your Bot's work; send messages as your Bot; send and receive files; what it asks you for; the hosts it talks to; that it runs confined), official or not, with the main action Allow and install. An update that asks for more opens the same dialog, says so and lists only what is new. A reviewed adapter plugin's update that asks for nothing new and changes no program by a major version applies by itself in the background through the same update job (never one a system installer would put in place, which asks for a password, nor one while a computer made with it is running); everything else shows Update and waits. An installed channel's row adds Manage: one `lg` dialog named for the plugin listing each connected Bot (name, account handle and owner in the meta, one state tag showing Connecting as a working ring and a problem in words, ··· menu: Get a pairing code…, Remove owner, Change token…, Disconnect… which asks), a notice while nobody is paired or the service stopped accepting the token, and under it that connection's Chats: those waiting for the owner first, each with Allow and ··· Rules… / Block; a blocked chat is dimmed. Rules… (`ChatRulesDialog`) applies each change as made and has Done only: who can talk to the Bot (Only you, or Everyone in the chat with a notice saying what that lets people do), Only when addressed for a group, and where messages go (the Bot answers in the chat, or only Routines). Connect a Bot is its own dialog (the Bot, the token as a password field with its steps behind an ⓘ), then the pairing dialog showing the code in two groups with Copy command, how long it lasts, and Paired once the owner has sent it. A Routine's trigger menu offers When a message arrives wherever a Bot has an account: the account, one chat or any, and words the message must contain, with a notice when the chosen chat sends its messages to the Bot rather than to Routines.
- Group settings by user goals. Distinguish global defaults from Bot overrides, subscriptions from custom keys, and plugin installation from account connection. Ordinary setting descriptions never expose internal protocol or database field names.
